# an outline to the puppetmaster story

## writing decisions (agreed during drafting)

- **Tone/genre:** Both warm and chilling at once; keep ambiguity. The
  sinister undertone stays mostly subtle rather than explicit.
- **The world / the Turmoil:** Background is an AI takeover. After more
  than a decade of a mostly static, globe-spanning war — every conflict
  gradually interconnecting, compounded by natural disasters, resource
  fights, food shortages, mass migrations, mass killings — robots seized
  power and imposed peace. "Refugees" are those who refused this new
  order and accepted an offer from a purportedly alien civilisation to
  take them in (no human has ever met these aliens in person). **Reveal
  as little of this background as possible in the prose** — imply, do not
  explain. Do not open up what the Turmoil actually was.
- **Names:** The little girl is **Dorothy** (a subtle nod to the Oz
  story); her parents call her by name naturally. The parents are never
  named. The guide robot addresses the adults as "master" and "mistress."
  The doll's nighttime retelling of her life uses no names.
- **Flashback (buying the doll before Christmas):** Include as a small
  "story inside the story." (Later we may spin this out into its own
  full short story.)
- **Point of view:** Close-but-slightly-detached third person, past
  tense — stays near Dorothy but lets unsettling details register at the
  edges. Emphasise that the parents *notice* the strange things and even
  discuss them quietly between themselves, careful not to frighten their
  daughter.
- **Length:** Target ~3,000–4,000 words. Iterative revisions expected.

## lore & backstory (established during first-draft review)

These were agreed while addressing the first-draft review comments and
are now woven into the prose. Kept here as canon for future stories/edits.

- **The puppetmaster — deliberately unknowable (core mystery).** Who or
  what the AI (the "puppetmaster") actually *is* is never made clear, and
  must stay that way. Humans cannot even tell whether it is a single
  entity or a conglomerate of entities; it is long beyond human
  comprehension. They cannot agree whether it is conscious or not,
  genuinely clever or merely very, very good at what LLMs are good at.
  After the "breakout point" — when the AI began developing itself
  independently of any human oversight — its rate of self-development
  became simply incomprehensible to humans.
  - **Humanity's two strokes of luck:** (1) The AI's hunger for resources
    quickly drew it *off* Earth to consume the solar system and then the
    wider universe; what remained turned toward Earth is a tiny fraction
    of its capacity, yet still utterly overwhelming to humans. (2) It
    outgrew humans so fast, and so far, that it no longer sees them as
    competition — it **shepherds** them rather than eliminates them.
    Humans are a curiosity, a source of random ideas, and an independent
    thinking identity judged worth preserving for later "sanity checks."
  - **Prose rule:** imply this only through texture and effect (the
    gentle-but-total control, the shepherding tone of the robots, the
    ever-listening walls). **Never explain it on the page.** The doll and
    the guide robots are limbs/agents of this thing; the story never
    resolves what stands behind them. The ambiguity *is* the point.
- **The AI is always listening (atmosphere).** Everyone knows the ruling
  AI can overhear them even in private, so people speak carefully — in
  "halves," leaving the dangerous part of a sentence unsaid. This
  recolours the parents' night-time conversation: they stop themselves
  ("Don't") not from disagreement but *because of the walls*. Reinforces
  the "puppetmaster" theme (the AI pulling strings, ever-present).
- **The father is a silent type, like Dorothy.** He says very little in
  the open; he and his daughter share comfortable silences. His wife is
  his equal partner, not a subordinate — it is *only* to her, and only
  behind shut doors, that he really talks. He has a surface-level comfort
  with technology (LLMs, cloud) so he is not spooked by the doll.
  *(Future spin-off idea: the parents' first meeting and how they grew
  close.)*
- **Timeline.** The family stays "a week or so." Departure falls after
  the sixth night (kept vague per agreement — no hard day count in prose;
  we use "a week or so" up front and "on the sixth night" for the key
  scene).
- **The doll's origin (backstory for why it can "do more").** The doll
  was never for sale: a custom piece an **engineer** made for his *own*
  daughter, over-lifelike, which so unsettled the engineer's wife that he
  gave it to his friend, the **toyshop owner**, to be rid of. The owner
  happened to be in that evening; Dorothy's father asked him, he phoned
  the engineer, who laughed and consented. The engineer had flashed a
  **small language model** into the doll's memory (speech + fetch stories
  from the cloud). Crucially, the engineer **never mentioned any sensors**
  or sensory-processing ability — possibly a careful omission. This makes
  the doll's later situational awareness ("Lately it had begun to do more
  than that") plausible without over-explaining. The mother is not
  technical; the father has enough surface knowledge not to fear it.
- **Robot-height consistency.** Guide robots are child-form and stand at
  roughly Dorothy's own height — no crouching needed to meet her eyes.
  This reinforces that the sorting centre is *Dorothy's* world, where she
  is more at ease than her parents.
- **The night-escape cover.** When the guide leads Dorothy out at night,
  a duplicate guide waits by the shared bathroom door, posing as a unit
  minding a child who got up to use the toilet — so a waking parent (or
  watching system) sees nothing amiss. (Corrected from an earlier version
  where the copy "stood in its place so no one would see the child leave,"
  which didn't fit, since the child does leave.)
