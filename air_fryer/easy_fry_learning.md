## Before the first cooking session

Historical status: first-use cleaning was confirmed completed before
heating on 2026-10-17. The almond trial below has since been completed.
The following advice is retained as reference, not as outstanding setup work.

Do not use food to remove the appliance's new-appliance smell. Dry almonds
can still pick up odours; a lack of surface moisture does not protect them
from airborne substances.

Tefal's instructions say to remove packaging and stickers, wash the bowl
with hot water, washing-up liquid and a non-abrasive sponge, and wipe the
appliance's interior and exterior with a damp cloth. Dry before use; do not
immerse the main unit. Use a ventilated kitchen and a heat-resistant surface,
with at least 15 cm clearance around the appliance, not under a wall cupboard.

The supplied manual does not specify a mandatory empty burn-in or its
time/temperature. Follow any additional first-use directions supplied with
the exact model rather than assuming a generic 200 C / 20 minute cycle.
If a strong chemical/burning smell or smoke persists, stop, unplug, let it
cool, and consult Tefal before cooking food.

## Trial 1: plain, dry-roasted almonds (completed 2026-10-17)

The table below preserves the proposed starting settings, not a confirmed
record of the settings actually used and not a Tefal-tested almond recipe.

| Item | Starting plan |
| --- | --- |
| Almonds | 200 g Alesto raw, whole almonds |
| Preparation | Straight from the packet, provided labelled ready to eat; no soaking, peeling, rinsing or oil needed |
| Load | Spread in a single layer; if 200 g will not fit without piling up, use two batches |
| Preset | Cookies, then override both temperature and time; do not use Dehydration |
| Temperature | 160 C |
| Preheat | 3 minutes at 160 C, empty, after first-use preparation; this is for a repeatable starting point, not a burn-in |
| Initial cooking timer | 8 minutes, counted after adding the almonds |
| Shake/stir | After 4 minutes |
| First check | After 6 minutes; stop early if already sufficiently roasted |
| Adjustment | If needed after 8 minutes, add 1 minute at a time; roughly 8-11 minutes total is a guide, not a target to reach regardless of colour |
| Cooling | Immediately transfer to a heatproof plate/tray in one layer; cool about 15-20 minutes, until completely cool |

Check the packet's preparation instructions first; they take priority if it
requires cooking. Remove any damaged or suspect nuts. Keep the first trial
plain so seasoning does not obscure the baseline flavour.

Choose the Cookies preset through the cooking-mode selector, change the
temperature to 160 C and timer to the desired duration, then start. Tefal
lists Cookies among the adjustable cooking modes; it has no almond preset.
The preset is a convenient starting point, not an almond-specific programme.

Look for a toasted, nutty smell and slightly darker skins, not deep browning.
Split a sample after letting it cool enough to handle: the centre should
look lightly toasted. Almonds are softer while hot and become crisp as they
cool, so do not keep roasting just to make them crunchy in the basket.
Let a sample cool before tasting. Cool the batch fully before airtight storage.

## Closing remarks and next trials

The pleasant aroma is encouraging. White to lightly browned centres suggest
a relatively light roast with some variation, but colour alone does not
establish whether the flavour and crunch are ideal. Compare several almonds
after complete cooling before deciding that the batch needs a darker roast.
A uniformly brown centre is not a necessary goal.

For the next batch, keep the same weight, layer depth, preset, preheat and
temperature, and extend the actual roasting time by just 1 minute. If this
trial used 160 C for 8 minutes, the comparison would be 160 C for 9 minutes.
Record the actual baseline first; do not assume the planned timer was the
final cooking time. Further trials can add another minute if the cooled
result still tastes too lightly roasted and there are no scorched or bitter
notes.

Try more time before more heat: a higher temperature can darken the outside
faster without improving centre evenness. As a separate comparison, try
165 C (or the next available setting above 160 C) at the original cooking
time, checking early. Do not increase both time and temperature in the same
comparison, and do not treat extra time as a fix for uneven loading.

To judge the interior, remove a few almonds from different parts of the bowl
near the end, let them cool enough to handle, and split them. Let samples
cool fully before comparing flavour and crunch. Use aroma and the window as
supporting clues, not a substitute for sampling. Keep a single layer and
redistribute the almonds thoroughly halfway through; if variation persists,
try an additional stir or a smaller batch as its own experiment.

## Storing the cooled roasted almonds

Let the almonds cool completely before sealing them. A clean, completely
dry glass jar or food-storage container with a tight-fitting lid is a good
default. The original resealable packet is also suitable if it is clean,
dry, undamaged and still seals well; press out excess air before closing.
Do not return warm nuts to the packet. Label the roasting date so roasted
and unroasted nuts are not confused.

Keep a small working supply in a cool, dark cupboard away from the fryer,
sunlight and steam. For this first small batch, aiming to use it within
1-2 weeks is a practical quality target, not a measured shelf life or safety
deadline. For longer keeping, refrigerate or freeze in airtight, preferably
small portions to limit exposure to moisture and other foods' odours.
Let a chilled/frozen portion warm while still sealed before opening, to
reduce condensation on the nuts.

Roasting does not rescue stale/rancid nuts or reset the packet's storage
history. Discard nuts with mould, rancid/off odours or an unpleasant rancid
flavour. Storage conditions and the starting product matter more than a
generic recipe-page expiry estimate.

Sources: [Airfryfoods](https://airfryfoods.com/how-to-roast-nuts-in-an-air-fryer/)
recommends complete cooling, airtight containers or resealable bags, and
refrigeration/freezing for longer storage.
[Utah State University Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/archive/how-to-roast-nuts-and-seeds)
also specifies cooling before airtight storage; its longer published
storage estimates are not a verified shelf life for this batch.

## Chapter 2: walnuts and cashews (planned)

Status: **planned, not tested**. No results or successful settings are
established for either nut in this appliance.

### Aim and source assessment

Find a plain, lightly roasted baseline for each nut separately before
trying mixed nuts or seasoning. The available walnuts and cashews have not
yet been confirmed as raw/unroasted, unsalted, whole/halved or broken;
check their labels and condition first. These plans assume edible,
store-bought, shelled, unroasted kernels. Already-roasted nuts need a
different plan.

The [Airfryfoods article](https://airfryfoods.com/how-to-roast-nuts-in-an-air-fryer/)
is useful for single-layer loading, optional oil, stirring and storage.
Its main recipe gives 150 C for 10-15 minutes, whereas its FAQ says
5-12 minutes and its statements about walnuts' relative cooking time vary.
It is not a Tefal-specific trial. Do not adopt its longest time without
checking, and do not treat preheating or seasoning as universally essential.

### Proposed trials: walnuts-001 and cashews-001

These conservative inspection schedules are a synthesis, not timings
verified by the article or Tefal. Cooking time starts after loading and
excludes preheat.

| Parameter | walnuts-001 | cashews-001 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Walnut halves of similar size; separate small fragments | Whole cashews of similar size; separate broken pieces |
| Mass | 100 g, or less if needed for a single layer; record actual mass | 100 g, or less if needed for a single layer; record actual mass |
| Preparation | Plain, dry; no added oil, salt or soaking | Plain, dry; no added oil, salt or soaking |
| Preset | Cookies, override both values | Cookies, override both values |
| Temperature | 150 C | 150 C |
| Preheat | 3 min at 150 C | 3 min at 150 C |
| Initial timer | 4 min | 6 min |
| First inspection and gentle redistribution | At 3 min | At 3 min |
| If more roasting is needed | Add 1 min at a time, checking each time | Add 1 min at a time, checking each time |
| Stop criteria | Pleasant toasted aroma and desired light roast; stop before dark/bitter edges | Pleasant toasted aroma and light golden patches; stop before dark/bitter spots |
| After cooking | Spread on a heatproof tray and cool fully before judging | Spread on a heatproof tray and cool fully before judging |

The initial timers are checkpoints, not promises of a finished roast.
Walnut crevices and thin edges can darken unevenly; cashews can also scorch
in patches. Sample more than one piece, cool enough to handle before
splitting, and compare fully cooled flavour and texture. Do not aim for
uniform deep browning. If broken nuts must be used, make a separate,
earlier-checked trial rather than mixing them with large pieces.

Do not roast walnuts and cashews together in these first trials. A second
batch in an already-hot appliance is not a cold-start comparison: either
allow the appliance to cool between trials or record the hot start and
check earlier. Use the guide's trial-record template, preserve actual
times/settings, and change only one variable in each later comparison.
Use the same complete-cooling and airtight-storage approach as for almonds.
