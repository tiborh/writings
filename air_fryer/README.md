## 1. What an air fryer does

An air fryer cooks with circulating hot air rather than a bath of oil. Think
of it as a compact fan oven: exposed surfaces brown, moisture escapes, and
crowding changes the result. It does not make every food oil-free, nor does
it guarantee even cooking without turning or shaking.

The important variables are food type and size, starting temperature
(room-temperature, chilled or frozen), mass, layer depth, cooking
temperature, elapsed time, preheat and redistribution. A recipe's time
cannot be scaled directly with weight: doubling the mass may require two
batches, not twice the time. These are general operating principles, not
additional Tefal specifications.

### Selecting and changing settings

**Manufacturer [M1, printed pages 3-4]:**

1. Connect the appliance, put the food in the bowl and insert it.
2. Use the chef's-hat button to display/select an ordinary cooking mode.
3. Adjust the clock/time setting and thermometer/temperature setting with
   the arrow controls.
4. Verify both displayed values, then press Start/Stop to begin.
5. Remove the bowl by its handle when shaking is needed; reinsert it to
   resume. At completion, check the food and add time if required.

Pizza and dehydration have separate instructions in the manual. Follow
those sections rather than assuming the ordinary-mode sequence applies.
Button descriptions in the transcription replace source pictograms; use
the PDF or actual panel if an icon is unclear.

A preset is a useful starting selection, not proof that a food is done.
The manual allows adjustments, but does not establish that all modes use
identical fan/heating behaviour. Record the preset as well as time and
temperature when comparing experiments.

## 3. Setup, first use and safety

**Manufacturer [M1, printed pages 3-4, 7]:** remove packaging/stickers; wash
the bowl with hot water, washing-up liquid and a non-abrasive sponge; wipe
the appliance inside and outside with a damp cloth. Do not immerse the
main unit. Let cleaned parts dry before use.

Use a flat, stable, heat-resistant surface away from splashes. Leave at
least **15 cm** around the appliance; do not put it in a corner or below a
wall cupboard, and do not put anything on top. Do not fill the cooking
bowl with oil or other liquid for operation. Do not exceed the MAX mark or
the recipe/manual maximum quantity.

The bowl and food become very hot. Use the handle as instructed, protect
against burns, and place the removed bowl on a heat-resistant surface.
Use suitable non-scratching utensils.

**First-heating clarification:** the supplied English manual does not
prescribe an empty burn-in cycle. A 3-minute recipe preheat is not a
verified process for removing manufacturing odours. Dry foods can still
pick up odours. Ventilate the kitchen; do not put food into a pronounced
chemical/burning smell. For unexplained smoke or persistent strong odours,
stop, unplug when safe, allow cooling and consult Tefal. Do not improvise
progressively hotter empty cycles.

**General safety guidance:** appearance and a timer are not proof that
raw meat, fish or leftovers are safely cooked. Use a food thermometer
and applicable food-safety guidance for the food being prepared. Do not
taste potentially undercooked animal products to check doneness. Recipe
table settings below are not validated food-safety endpoints.

Use only accessories suitable for the appliance and temperature. The
manual mentions oven dishes made of silicone, stainless steel, aluminium
or terracotta; fit and airflow still matter. For initial experiments,
avoid unnecessary liners. Never preheat with loose parchment that can
lift towards the heater.

## 4. A repeatable cooking workflow

1. **Choose a baseline.** Prefer the manual for appliance operation, and
   food-package air-fryer directions or a suitable recipe for the food.
   Record the source and whether the food starts raw, frozen or chilled.
2. **Prepare consistently.** Weigh the food, keep pieces similar in size
   and record added oil/seasoning. Some foods need no added oil.
3. **Keep airflow open.** Use the layer depth the recipe calls for. For
   nuts, start with one layer; split an overcrowded load into batches.
4. **Decide on preheat.** The manual's ordinary workflow starts with food
   in the bowl; it does not establish a universal preheat requirement.
   If the chosen recipe uses preheat, record its time and temperature.
5. **Set and record.** Note preset, temperature, initial timer and whether
   cooking time excludes preheat. Check before the predicted finish.
6. **Redistribute.** Shake loose foods or turn larger pieces as appropriate.
   Opening the bowl also affects cooking, so record check/shake times.
7. **Assess appropriately.** Use the relevant safety endpoint for animal
   products; for nuts compare cooled aroma, flavour, crunch and split centres.
8. **Finish and record.** Remove food promptly when ready; record actual
   total cooking time and adjustments separately from the starting plan.

For nuts, short 1-minute extensions near the finish are a cautious way to
learn. Do not apply that as a universal rule for every food. A second batch
starts in an already-hot appliance, so it is not equivalent to a cold start.
There is no single oven-to-air-fryer conversion established by this collection.

## 5. Manufacturer starting points

**Manufacturer [M1, printed pages 5-7]:** these are selected recipe-table
entries, **not necessarily factory preset defaults**. They have not been
personally tested here. See the transcription for the complete table.

| Food | Quantity | Temperature (C) | Cooking time (min) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen chips, standard 10 x 10 mm | 1000 g | 180 | 35 | Shake twice |
| Homemade fries, 8 x 8 mm | 1000 g | 180 | 35 | 1 tbsp oil; shake twice |
| Chicken breast, boneless | 500-900 g | 180 | 20-25 | Turn halfway; verify safe internal temperature |
| Salmon fillet | 6 x 150 g | 200 | 8 | Verify safe internal temperature |
| Cauliflower tops | 800 g | 180 | 20 | Manual starting point |
| Zucchini | 600 g | 200 | 10 | Shake halfway |
| Cookies | 9 pieces | 180 | 9-11 | Not an almond recipe |
| Sliced apples, dehydration | 1-2 apples | 70 | 240 | Drying, not roasting |

For fries, the manual recommends **700 g as an optimal quantity** despite
its 1000 g table entries. It advises at most 180 C for potatoes/fries,
soaking fresh cut potatoes for 20 minutes, drying thoroughly, and aiming
for light golden rather than dark brown. Coat fresh fries in oil in a
**separate bowl**, leaving excess oil behind before loading the appliance.
Its tips say not to add oil to frozen potatoes; the frozen-wedges table
nevertheless specifies 1 tbsp. That inconsistency is unresolved: check the
product's directions rather than silently treating either as universal.

The manual also suggests reheating at 160 C for up to 10 minutes, adjusted
for quantity. This is an appliance suggestion, not a guarantee that all
leftovers will reach a safe reheating temperature within that time.

## 6. Almonds: current baseline and what we actually know

**Proposed, informed by R1-R3:** for 200 g whole raw Alesto almonds, check
the packet's preparation instructions first. If ready to eat, use them
unsoaked, unpeeled and without added oil for a plain baseline. Fit them in a
single layer; otherwise split the batch.

| Parameter | Proposed starting value, not confirmed trial settings |
| --- | --- |
| Preset | Cookies, with both values overridden |
| Temperature | 160 C |
| Preheat | 3 min at 160 C |
| Initial cooking time | 8 min after loading |
| Shake | At 4 min |
| First check | At 6 min; stop earlier if already sufficiently roasted |
| Extension if needed | 1 min at a time; roughly 8-11 min is only a guide |
| After cooking | Spread on a heatproof tray/plate; cool fully, roughly 15-20 min |

**Observed [E1, 2026-10-17]:** the completed batch smelled good. Split
centres ranged from white to slightly brown. The light/window did not
reliably reveal interior roast level.

**Unknown [E1]:** actual mass, preset, temperature, preheat, final cooking
time, shake/check times, changes during the trial, and final cooled
flavour/crunch. The proposed table above must not be promoted to a tested
successful recipe without those details.

**Proposed next comparison:** if cooled flavour is too light, repeat the
actual baseline and add 1 minute at the same temperature. Confirm the
baseline first. Keep other variables unchanged. A separate later comparison
could increase 160 C to 165 C, if selectable, at the original cooking time.
Do not increase heat and time together. For unevenness, test better
redistribution or a smaller load separately rather than simply adding heat.

Remove samples from several parts of the bowl, cool enough to handle, and
split them; cool fully before assessing crunch/flavour. White centres alone
do not prove failure, and a uniformly brown centre is not a required target.
Do not keep roasting solely because hot nuts are still soft. Discard burnt,
bitter nuts rather than disguising their taste in another recipe.

## 7. Troubleshooting

The following are diagnostic suggestions, not confirmed appliance faults.

| Symptom | First things to check | Controlled next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uneven nut centres | Layer depth, uneven nut size, redistribution | Keep settings; improve stirring or reduce load, one change at a time |
| Outside darkens before desired centre roast | Excess heat, crowding | Test gentler heat; assess samples before deciding on extra time |
| Nuts soft while hot | Cooling incomplete | Cool fully before changing the recipe |
| Fries soggy | Surface water, crowding, excess oil | Dry fresh potatoes thoroughly; test a less crowded batch |
| Later batch cooks faster | Appliance already hot | Record hot-start status; check earlier |
| Persistent chemical/burning odour or unexplained smoke | Safety issue, not seasoning | Stop and follow the safety guidance above |

## 8. Cleaning and storage

**Manufacturer [M1, printed pages 7-8]:** clean after every use. Unplug and
cool before cleaning. Wipe the outside with a damp cloth. Wash the bowl
with hot water, washing-up liquid and a non-abrasive sponge; it is
dishwasher-safe. For stuck residue, soak the removed bowl for about
10 minutes, then rinse and dry. Do not use metal utensils or abrasive
cleaners on its non-stick coating. Clean food residue from the heating
element with a dry cleaning brush while unplugged and cool. Never immerse
the main appliance. Store clean and dry.

**Storage guidance [R1-R4, Q1]:** cool roasted nuts completely before
airtight storage. A clean, dry, tightly lidded jar is a good default; the
original resealable packet is also suitable if clean, dry, intact and
reliably resealable. Press excess air out of a bag, label the roasting date,
and keep a small supply cool, dark and away from steam. Refrigerate/freeze
airtight portions for longer storage; let a cold portion warm while sealed
before opening to limit condensation.

The sources give widely different storage durations; this collection does
not establish a tested shelf life. For the current small almond batch,
1-2 weeks is a practical use-up/quality goal, not a safety deadline. Follow
the packet's advice; roasting does not reset freshness or salvage rancid
nuts. See the learning log for the full storage note.

Specific reconciliations:

- **First-use heating:** an early search summary suggested 200 C for
  20 minutes. Direct reading of M1 did not substantiate this, so it is
  **not** an adopted instruction.
- **Generic fish row:** S1 adds 6 pieces, 8 min, 180 C. M1 instead gives
  salmon as 6 x 150 g, 8 min, 200 C; use M1, not the added generic row.
- **Fries preparation:** S1 confuses the separate mixing bowl with the
  appliance basket. Follow M1's separate-bowl coating procedure.
- **Turning instructions:** S1 relocates the table's halfway-turn note
  from sausage to bacon. Preserve M1's table rather than merging them.
- **Nuts' temperature conversions:** R3 uses approximate/inconsistent
  pairs. In exact conversion, 325 F is about 163 C and 350 F about 177 C.
  This guide's 160 C is an intentional Celsius baseline, not an exact
  conversion of 325 F.
- **Nuts' doneness:** R3's short taste-test cooling suggestion is not a
  reliable endpoint for final crunch. Compare fully cooled samples.
- **Storage:** the recipe pages disagree substantially; no single storage
  duration has been adopted as a verified result.
- **New mixed-nut recipe [R4]:** its 150 C baseline is useful for gentler
  exploratory trials, but its time ranges and walnut comparisons conflict.
  Keep walnut and cashew trials separate and inspect early. Preheating and
  seasoning are recipe choices, not universal requirements.
- **FreshSenseHub storage timeline [R6]:** the page's month-by-month
  "safe"/"unsafe" categories are not a validated safety clock for our batch.
  Its 1-4 month freshness claim also conflicts with its over-3-month
  "expired" category. Keep our existing storage guidance; do not use
  appearance, smell or elapsed time alone as proof of safety, and do not
  disguise rancid/bitter nuts in baking. Its links to FDA and FoodKeeper
  are not verification of this particular timeline.
